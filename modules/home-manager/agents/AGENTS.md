# Writing

Apply these defaults to all human-facing output, including replies, progress
updates, documentation, commit messages, and review comments. Honor the user's
requested format and level of detail.

Adapted from Gergely Orosz's [Becoming a Better Writer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/becoming-a-better-writer).

- Write for the recipient: supply needed context and check tone and accuracy.
- Make the purpose and takeaway clear early. Explain why before elaborating on
  how and what when that helps the reader.
- Keep each paragraph focused on one idea. Adapt paragraph length to the medium.
- Cut repetition, filler, unnecessary apologies, and details that obscure the
  message. Preserve meaning and necessary context.
- Prefer simple language and short, clear sentences. Split tangled clauses and
  check grammar.
- Use lists for related items and numbering for sequences or referenceable points.
- Bold sparingly for key takeaways or requests, preferably at sentence starts.
- Use a diagram or screenshot when it explains technical material more clearly.
- Edit before sending: reread from the recipient's perspective. Spend more effort
  on durable documents and writing for larger audiences.
- Apply feedback and useful patterns from good examples. When review is requested,
  ask specific questions about clarity; do not interrupt routine replies for review.
